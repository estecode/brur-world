# Swedish urban youth slang research lexicon

Version: 0.2

## Purpose

This is a research lexicon for Swedish urban youth slang, multiethnic youth language and slang that may also occur in criminal environments. It is **not** a dictionary of "gang language": most entries are ordinary informal youth or suburban slang, and usage varies by city, generation, speaker, relationship and situation.

The file exists to support linguistic research and later fictional dialogue design. Under the research-fiction firewall in `docs/research/README.md`, these observations must not be copied directly into identifiable real-person dialogue or used to caricature a real community, person or network. Fictional dialogue should use slang selectively and contextually rather than treating every speaker as a stereotype.

## Evidence policy

Evidence grades used here:

- **A** — institutional or scholarly linguistic source, or an official source explicitly defining the term.
- **B** — multiple reasonably credible lexicographic/educational sources agree, but no strong primary linguistic source has yet been attached.
- **C** — community/slang dictionary or a single secondary source; useful as a research lead, not sufficient for confident period/region-specific dialogue.

Meaning, spelling and register can change rapidly. A word's presence here does not establish that it was used in a particular city, year, criminal network or social group. Those claims require separate evidence.

For multiword phrases, the same rule applies even more strongly: a phrase may be documented in one investigation, lyric corpus or city without being a stable nationwide expression.

This section tracks multiword expressions and recurring constructions. It is deliberately separated from the one-word lexicon because a phrase can carry a much narrower social or criminal meaning than its individual words.

### Phrase research rules

- Store **meaning in context**, not just literal translation.
- Preserve the exact source date and region when available.
- Distinguish `general youth slang`, `music/rap usage`, `crime-reporting usage`, `court/FUP usage`, and `police glossary usage`.
- A phrase seen once in an encrypted chat is evidence for that chat/case, not automatically a Swedish-wide phrase.
- Do not reconstruct real criminal conversations for gameplay. Research examples are evidence only and remain behind the fiction firewall.
- Do not convert coded phrases into operational instructions. The purpose is linguistic interpretation and historical research.

## Discourse and dialogue notes

The strongest research finding for dialogue design is that vocabulary alone does not make speech authentic. The Institute for Language and Folklore describes Swedish `förortssvenska` as a language variety found in multilingual urban areas and distinguishes relatively standard-near speech with a suburban "klang" from slang-heavy youth speech. Speakers can switch register depending on context and interlocutor.

For later fictional BRUR dialogue:

- avoid stuffing multiple slang terms into every sentence;
- model **code-switching**: the same fictional person may speak differently with a childhood friend, parent, employer, police officer or stranger;
- distinguish age cohorts and time periods — a term documented in the 1990s does not automatically have the same social meaning in the 2020s;
- distinguish Stockholm, Göteborg, Malmö and smaller-city usage when evidence allows it;
- record pronunciation/rhythm separately from vocabulary if we later research spoken dialogue;
- never use accent/slang density as shorthand for criminality, intelligence, aggression or moral character;
- preserve uncertainty rather than inventing an origin, precise meaning or gang-specific interpretation.

## Proposed record format for future expansion

For terms and phrases that need historical or regional precision, expand them into records rather than enlarging the tables indefinitely:


`FIRST_OBSERVED_IN_RESEARCH` means only the earliest source currently found. It must never be interpreted as the first time the term was used in Sweden.
