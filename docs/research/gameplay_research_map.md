# BRUR Gameplay Research Map

Status: v0.2 — living research document

## Purpose

This document maps recurring patterns reported in Swedish news about criminal networks during roughly 2011–2026 into possible gameplay capabilities and consequence systems for BRUR.

It is research input, not a plan to recreate specific real crimes, victims, perpetrators, or organizations. Real cases are used to identify general patterns that can inform fictional systems and scenarios.

## Core design principle

> **BRUR depicts criminal environments without romanticizing them. Violence and crime may be central gameplay mechanics, but they should have credible human, social, and material consequences.**

Player agency should remain broad. The game should not become a moral lecture or make criminal gameplay artificially unplayable. Consequences should primarily emerge from the world, its systems, relationships, law enforcement, and the longer-term effects of player actions.

### Special case: violence against relatives

This behavior needs careful dating because different forms emerged at different times.

**Threats and explosions against relatives:** clearly established no later than **2020** in the current source set. Swedish Radio reported that explosions and death threats from criminal networks were often aimed at family members of the actual target.

**Relatives deliberately substituted as shooting targets:** **early 2023** is currently the strongest media breakpoint. Reporting in March 2023 described several shootings where relatives or friends were suspected to be the intended targets, and the deliberate targeting of relatives was described as a possible new development.

**Systematic escalation:** by **September–October 2023**, reporting describes relatives as increasingly common targets. Police were risk-assessing more than a thousand people and many family-linked addresses because of feared attacks.

Research implication: BRUR should not flatten all violence against relatives into one timeless mechanic. Threats, address attacks, explosions and deliberate shootings have different media histories and should remain separately tagged.

## Time development to preserve

BRUR should not model gang-related crime as one static phenomenon.

### 2011–2014

Local conflicts, drug markets, firearms, shootings and silence around investigations are already visible. This period requires deeper archive research before precise first-observed dates are treated as high confidence.

### 2015–2016: public firearm violence and grenade attacks

Automatic weapons, high-profile public shootings, hand grenades and attacks against residential addresses become especially visible in reporting. The risk to people who are not the intended target is an important consequence-system marker.

### 2017–2019: recurring violence and attacks on institutions

Reporting includes repeated attacks on police buildings/property while shootings and explosions remain major criminal-violence phenomena. More research is needed on the evolution of drug-market organization, extortion and economic crime during this interval.

### 2020–2022: organizational structures become unusually visible

Encrypted-communication investigations expose weapons storage, kidnapping, extortion, planned explosions, hierarchy and coercion. Violence and threats against relatives are already documented as pressure methods. Criminal markets and network activity are increasingly reported beyond the largest cities.

### 2023: distributed conflict and relatives as direct targets

This is a major behavioral breakpoint in the current research. Relatives increasingly become direct targets of shootings as well as address-based violence; very young executors become highly visible; leaders can direct activity from abroad; conflicts and network presence spread geographically.

### 2024–2025: digital recruitment and a market for execution

Recruitment can occur nationally without an established personal relationship. Social media and encrypted groups connect organizers to young potential executors, and journalistic investigations expose offers of murder and explosion assignments through digital channels.

### 2026

Do not assume that every previous trend continues upward. Later research must include declines, adaptation by law enforcement, changes in methods, prevention, exit from criminal networks and displacement into other forms of crime.

## Emerging gameplay loop

Avoid a simple loop such as:

```text
commit crime -> earn money -> wanted level -> escape -> reset -> repeat
```

A more appropriate systemic direction is:

```text
need / belonging / money
        -> contacts
        -> small criminal tasks
        -> debt / loyalty / status
        -> greater responsibility
        -> money / influence
        -> conflict
        -> violence
        -> police / retaliation
        -> consequences for relationships and surroundings
        -> arrests / losses / power vacuums / new conflicts
```

A high criminal reputation should therefore not simply mean "winning". It may create access, money and influence while simultaneously increasing obligations, enemies, police attention, fear in ordinary relationships, and risks to people around the player.

The anti-glorification principle should be expressed through these systems and consequences rather than through moralizing messages.

## Research dimensions to expand

Future versions should deepen the map along the full lifecycle rather than focusing only on spectacular violent events:

```text
recruitment
-> first tasks
-> money / status
-> drug economy
-> debts
-> organization / hierarchy
-> territory / markets
-> conflict
-> violence
-> retaliation
-> police investigation
-> arrest / prison
-> relatives / civilians
-> exit programs / leaving crime
-> power vacuum / succession
```

Parallel research tracks should cover police work, victims and families, ordinary life around affected areas, criminal finances, prison, people leaving criminal networks, witnesses, social services and prevention.

## Research rules

1. Prefer Swedish primary news reporting, public-service reporting, official statistics and court/police material where appropriate.
2. Separate reported fact from gameplay interpretation.
3. Record dates so historical changes are not flattened into one timeless model.
4. For behavior timelines, distinguish `first observed in research`, `emerging`, `established`, and `escalated`.
5. Never equate the earliest source currently found with the first occurrence in Sweden.
6. Add `confidence` and `earliest source found` as the timeline matures, and move dates when stronger earlier evidence is found.
7. Look for mundane structures and consequences, not only spectacular crimes.
8. Do not turn identifiable real victims or perpetrators into entertainment content.
9. Use real events to derive fictional systemic patterns rather than recreating individual crimes.
10. Continue to look for evidence that challenges the current model, including declining trends, successful prevention and people leaving criminal environments.
