# BRUR Police Dispatch, Priority and Command Research

Status: v0.1 — public operational-structure research layer
Coverage target: Sweden, current public structure with historical context where sourced
Last research update: 2026-09-12

## Purpose

This file records the public, non-sensitive structure of how Swedish police receive urgent incidents, prioritize them, create and manage händelserapporter, allocate available resources, communicate through command systems, and coordinate with other emergency services.

It complements `police_response_research.md`, which records what happened after specific real incidents. This document instead describes the **general public response model** around RLC, priority, dispatch, command and broad radio structure.

This is descriptive research only. It must **not** be used to infer or catalogue current patrol gaps, response blind spots, hidden deployment rules, covert identifiers, tactical radio procedures, exact local staffing patterns, protected infrastructure or evasion opportunities.

## Core public model

```text
112 / police observation / other report
                ↓
               RLC
                ↓
information gathering + assessment
                ↓
händelserapport / event state
                ↓
priority assignment
                ↓
available police resources allocated
                ↓
radio / Rakel / other communication
                ↓
patrols and other resources en route
                ↓
updates from field and callers
                ↓
priority / resources may be changed
                ↓
scene response → investigation / handoff / closure
```

The public sources consistently describe RLC as the regional operational hub for acute police response. Operators receive 112 calls and radio reports, gather information, document, prioritize and create an overview of available police resources. RLC then leads and distributes those resources across the region.

## Regionledningscentralen (RLC)

Publicly documented responsibilities include:

- receive and assess 112 calls requiring police intervention;
- receive radio reports from police personnel;
- gather additional information from callers and other sources;
- document the incident in police systems;
- assign and revise priority;
- maintain an overview of available intervention resources;
- dispatch and guide patrols;
- coordinate with yttre befäl and jourhavande förundersökningsledare;
- cooperate with SOS Alarm, ambulance, rescue services, county administrative boards and other actors;
- update and close händelserapporter;
- at serious incidents, provide initial operational leadership until the command structure develops further.

RLC-befäl works under the vakthavande befäl and acts as operational supervisor for operator teams. Public recruitment material states that RLC-befäl handles difficult prioritization, reprioritization, quality assurance and, at serious incidents, initial operational command of field resources.

## Source of incident / alarm

A police händelserapport can originate from several broad sources. Public annual-report material shows that a substantial share is police-initiated rather than generated by public emergency calls.

Research categories:

```text
SOURCE:
  112_SOS
  police_initiated
  phone_other
  radio_report
  partner_agency
  alarm_system
  witness_or_public_report
  other
  unknown
```

Historical public example from the 2021 annual report:

- 53% of händelserapporter were police-initiated;
- 24% came from 112 / SOS Alarm;
- 16% came from telephone;
- the remainder came from other communication.

These proportions are historical observations, not fixed gameplay probabilities.

## Priority structure

Public police documents describe priorities 1, 2, 3 and 5.

| Priority | Publicly documented meaning | Public time target / handling |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | immediate intervention | patrol sent / intervention immediately |
| **2** | urgent / skyndsamt | within about 20 minutes |
| **3** | intervention in near time | patrol ordered or another action within about 4 hours |
| **5** | non-acute matter | normally transferred to local police area for assessment/handling |

Important research rule: these are **priority/handling targets**, not a promise that every real incident receives a patrol within the target. Public internal-review material notes that some priority-3 cases were not resourced and that resource availability affects handling.

Priority should therefore be stored as a state that may change as information changes:

```text
PRIORITY_STATE:
  initial_priority
  current_priority
  changed_at
  change_reason_publicly_known
```

Never infer hidden decision rules beyond what public sources establish.

## Information gathered during acute calls

Public police descriptions say RLC operators collect information, assess, document and prioritize while dealing with callers. For research/game-design abstraction, relevant public information categories are:

```text
INCIDENT_INFORMATION:
  location
  what_happened
  ongoing_or_completed
  injuries_reported
  weapons_reported
  suspect_description_if_reported
  direction_of_travel_if_reported
  number_of_people_involved_if_reported
  immediate_danger
  vulnerable_people_present
  caller_updates
  field_updates
```

This is a high-level information model only. It is not intended to recreate police questioning scripts or tactical decision trees.

## Dynamic prioritization and resource allocation

RLC does not operate as a simple table where one offence automatically equals one fixed number of patrols. Public sources describe operators and RLC-befäl as continuously assessing incoming information, available resources and competing incidents.

A research abstraction should therefore model:

```text
reported_severity
+ whether incident is ongoing
+ reported injuries
+ reported weapons
+ immediate danger
+ public-place impact
+ new caller / field information
+ currently available resources
                ↓
          priority decision
                ↓
        initial resource allocation
                ↓
       later escalation / reduction
```

Resource availability is a real factor. Police internal review has explicitly discussed cases where a patrol would likely have been assigned if more patrols had been available.

Do not turn this into a real-world predictor of police coverage or response.

## Broad resource categories

Public sources and incident reporting support the following broad categories without exposing tactical detail:

```text
RESOURCE_CATEGORY:
  patrol
  multiple_patrols
  yttre_befäl
  RLC_befäl
  vakthavande_befäl
  dog_unit
  traffic_police
  regional_specialist_resource
  bomb_or_explosive_expertise
  helicopter_when_publicly_reported
  ambulance
  rescue_service
  other_partner_agency
```

The number and combination should be recorded only when publicly documented for a specific historical incident.

Do not infer staffing formulas such as `incident type -> exact number of cars`.

## Example: major acute event

A 2026 police retrospective on the Uppsala triple murder describes the first call arriving at 17:04 and RLC immediately beginning work. Many callers generated a large flow of information; RLC personnel filtered and coordinated that information, maintained the operational picture and directed patrols. Police also noted that unusually many personnel were already available in Uppsala because of Valborg, allowing patrols to secure the scene and search for the perpetrator.

Research significance:

- the first report can trigger immediate RLC activity;
- simultaneous callers can rapidly change confidence and scale;
- information coordination is a major RLC function;
- available staffing at that moment can materially affect the response;
- the response can expand as the event becomes understood.

Do not use this single event as a universal deployment template.

## Rakel and radio structure

Rakel is Sweden's state-controlled digital radio/communications system for organizations responsible for public order, safety, health and defence.

Public MSB material establishes that:

- communication and coordination in Rakel take place in **talgrupper**;
- talgrupper can support communication within one organization or between different organizations;
- there are common national naming/numbering principles for shared groups;
- specific inter-agency structures support cooperation among rescue service, ambulance, police and SOS Alarm (RAPS);
- functional/name calls can be used instead of number calls in some inter-organizational settings.

The public national guideline lists generic regional police command call signs such as `Polis 1–0` through `Polis 8–0` for regional/national command functions. These public coordination identifiers are not evidence for how patrol-level identifiers should be represented.

For BRUR research, store radio only at a broad functional level:

```text
RADIO:
  SYSTEM: Rakel
  COMMUNICATION_TYPE:
    unit_to_RLC
    command_to_unit
    interagency_coordination
    field_update
  TALGRUPP_TYPE:
    police_internal
    shared_interagency
    unknown
  PUBLIC_CALLSIGN_IF_EXPLICITLY_PUBLISHED:
```

Do **not** build a catalogue of current local patrol call signs, internal talgrupper, encryption practice, covert identifiers or tactical radio routines.

## Safe abstraction for later BRUR design

Research data remains research data under the `docs/research/README.md` fiction firewall. No real dispatch event, real call sign, real priority history or real deployment pattern should become game data directly.

A safe future abstraction is:

```text
incident information state
        ↓
abstract urgency
        ↓
fictional dispatcher / command model
        ↓
fictional resource pressure
        ↓
fictional police response
```

The gameplay system may learn broad phenomena such as:

- incomplete information becomes clearer over time;
- urgent events can displace lower-priority work;
- multiple callers increase the information flow;
- a serious event can attract additional resources;
- command and inter-agency coordination become more complex as scale grows.

It must not reproduce real local availability, response gaps or tactical procedures.
